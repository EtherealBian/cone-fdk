# Three-dimensional curvature of the raw intensity around a pore — development only

Does raw CT curve upwards in many directions, as at the center of a dark compact void, or only along a few directions, as near a sheet? This test adds 3D shape information to the fixed local-profile tree recipe. It does not assume every hole is spherical or remove any component by shape. It does not use printed images, pre-trained weights or coordinates. Calibration centers remain z=[160,184), x=[360,616), y=[376,632) in 2/3/5. Native raw context (never labels) may extend five pixels beyond these bounds. Score only the familiar z=[256,352) crop. Do not use the other-depth replication slab for model development or inspect the formal/confirmation labels in this experiment.

At each voxel form B, the integer sum of the 27 original uint16 intensities in the 3x3x3 cube centered there. For r=1,2,4 take the 13 unoriented nonzero lattice directions d in {-1,0,1} cubed (pick the first nonzero coordinate positive). Compute (B(x+r*d)+B(x-r*d)-2*B(x))*(6/||d||^2) in signed integer arithmetic. At each radius sort all 13 responses and take ranks 1,4,7,10,13, then convert to float32 after multiplying in float64 by the published scan slope divided by (27*6*r*r). This describes the distribution of discrete second differences, not calibrated physical curvature. True source halo everywhere. Compare CUDA output for every new integer feature to independent array-based CPU sums and order statistics; also directly enumerate 27 raw samples at selected edges and an interior location per chunk. Test flat, linear, quadratic, high-contrast, axis reversal and slab splitting. An implementation mismatch stops prediction.
